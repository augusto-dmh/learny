# Autonomous / Self-Regulated Learning Science and AI-Powered Study Homes

Scope note: research run 2026-09-30. Several primary pages (PNAS, Nature, Wiley, openai.com, anthropic.com/news for one report) returned 403/404/redirects to WebFetch; where that happened I used the Stanford SCALE 2026 evidence review (which tabulates those RCTs), the PDF of the paper itself, or a secondary review, and I mark each such case. ## Key Question 1: Which techniques have the strongest, most replicated effects for independent learners studying from books, and which popular ones are weak?

### Takeaway
Practice testing (retrieval) and distributed (spaced) practice are the two techniques rated "high utility" by Dunlosky et al. 2013 and confirmed by every later meta-analysis (g roughly 0.5–0.85); self-explanation, elaborative interrogation, interleaving, generation, and teaching/explaining-to-others are medium-to-large and well replicated; highlighting, rereading, and untrained summarization are low-utility, and the reading-comprehension "systems" (SQ3R etc.) rest on weak, small classroom studies rather than rigorous trials.

### Cited Findings
- Dunlosky et al. 2013 (Psychological Science in the Public Interest, 10 techniques reviewed) rated practice testing and distributed practice as high utility; elaborative interrogation, self-explanation, and interleaved practice as moderate; and summarization, highlighting/underlining, keyword mnemonic, imagery for text, and rereading as low utility. Highlighting's "greatest problem is the variability in how learners use this technique"; rereading is "consistently inferior" to distributed practice, elaborative interrogation and self-explanation; summarization needs "considerable training before this technique optimizes learning". — [Dunlosky et al. 2013 PDF](http://iverson.cm.utexas.edu/courses/310M/Handouts/Dunlosky%20et%20al.%20-%202013%20-%20Improving%20Students%92%20Learning%20With%20Effective%20Learni.pdf); [AFT summary by Dunlosky](https://www.aft.org/ae/fall2013/dunlosky); [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26173288/)
- A 2023 meta-analysis organized around the Dunlosky framework reports distributed practice d = 0.85 (150 cases), practice testing d = 0.74 (374 cases), elaborative interrogation d = 0.56, imagery d = 0.56, self-explanation d = 0.54, with mnemonics, rereading, interleaving and underlining lower (per the search summary; numbers not re-verified against the paper body). — [Educational Psychology Review 2023](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-023-09809-2)
- Adesope, Trevisan & Sundararajan 2017 (272 effects, 118 experiments): overall testing effect g = 0.61; g = 0.93 vs. no activity, g = 0.51 vs. restudy/rereading; similar in lab (0.62) and classroom (0.67); retention 0.63 vs. transfer 0.53. — [Adesope et al. 2017](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/315706448_Rethinking_the_Use_of_Tests_A_Meta-Analysis_of_Practice_Testing)
- Yang et al. 2021 (Psychological Bulletin, classroom quizzing) found small-to-medium benefits for retention and transfer, smaller than Adesope's. — [Yang et al. 2021 PDF](https://gwern.net/doc/psychology/spaced-repetition/2021-yang.pdf)
- Roediger & Karpicke 2006: after one week, prose passages tested once recalled better than restudied (56% vs 42%); with study-test-test-test vs study×4 the one-week recall was 61% vs 40%. — [Roediger & Karpicke 2006, Psychological Science](https://colinallen.dnsalias.org/Readings/2006_Roediger_Karpicke_PsychSci.pdf); [Roediger & Karpicke 2006 PPS review](http://psychnet.wustl.edu/memory/wp-content/uploads/2018/04/Roediger-Karpicke-2006_PPS.pdf)
- Cepeda et al. 2006 meta-analysis of distributed practice in verbal recall: the inter-study interval producing maximal retention increases with the retention interval; the follow-up Cepeda et al. 2008 study puts the optimal gap near 10–20% of the retention interval (about 20% for tests weeks away, falling to about 5% at a one-year delay). — [Cepeda et al. 2006](https://www.yorku.ca/ncepeda/publications/CPVWR2006.html); [Cepeda et al. 2008 Psychological Science](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED505660.pdf)
- Successive relearning (retrieval to criterion, repeated across spaced days): in a difficult biopsychology course it raised exam performance by at least 10% (about one letter grade) with effect sizes 0.54–1.10; in a 56-day-spaced condition more than 75% of items were still recalled after one year and more than 60% after five years. — [Janes, Dunlosky, Rawson et al. 2020, Applied Cognitive Psychology](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/acp.3699); [Rawson & Dunlosky, APA Scholarship of Teaching and Learning](https://www.apa.org/pubs/journals/features/stl-0000024.pdf); [UNH successive relearning guide](https://www.unh.edu/teaching-learning-resource-hub/sites/default/files/media/2023-06/itow-successive-relearning-dunlosky-greve-badali-wissman-rawson.pdf)
- Interleaving: Rohrer, Dedrick, Hartwig & Cheung 2020 RCT (787 students, 54 classes, 4 months of interleaved vs blocked math assignments): on an unannounced test one month later interleaved scored 61% vs 38%, d = 0.83 [0.68, 0.97]; teachers implemented it without training. — [Rohrer et al. 2020, J. Educational Psychology](https://gwern.net/doc/psychology/spaced-repetition/2019-rohrer.pdf)
- Self-explanation: Bisra et al. 2018 meta-analysis (69 effects, 5,917 participants) g = 0.55 [0.45, 0.65] for prompted self-explanation. — [Bisra et al. 2018, Educational Psychology Review](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1186664)
- Learning by teaching / explain-back: Fiorella & Mayer's review found positive effects in 17 of 19 experiments, median d ≈ 0.71–0.77; Kobayashi 2019 meta-analysis of 28 studies: g = 0.35 for preparing-to-teach only, g = 0.56 for preparing plus actually teaching; teaching after study *without* teaching expectancy did not differ from zero. — [Kobayashi 2019, Japanese Psychological Research](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jpr.12221); [Fiorella "Learning by Teaching" overview](https://www.unh.edu/teaching-learning-resource-hub/sites/default/files/media/2023-06/itow-learning-by-teaching-fiorella.pdf)
- Productive failure / problem-solving-before-instruction: Sinha & Kapur 2021 meta-analysis (53 studies, 166 comparisons, >12,000 participants) g = 0.36 [0.20, 0.51] overall; g = 0.37–0.58 with high fidelity to productive-failure design; publication-bias-adjusted estimate g = 0.87; strongest for conceptual understanding and transfer. — [Sinha & Kapur 2021, Review of Educational Research](https://janfasen.nl/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/Sinha-and-Kapur-PS-I.pdf)
- Confidence-weighted practice tests (rate confidence per option) produced greater transfer to new but related questions than standard multiple-choice practice tests. — [Sparck, Bjork & Bjork 2016, Cognitive Research](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5256426/)
- Making judgments of learning after retrieval practice enhanced learning of educational texts to a medium extent relative to retrieval practice alone (minimal harmful reactivity). — [PMC10607076](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10607076/)
- Deans for Impact "The Science of Learning" (2nd ed.) is the practitioner synthesis of the above for teachers; it does not add new effect sizes. — [Deans for Impact PDF](https://www.deansforimpact.org/files/assets/thescienceoflearning2nded.pdf)
- SQ3R: the available studies are small classroom quasi-experiments, mostly in EFL settings (e.g., a comprehension score rising from 22.47 to 30.10 vs a conventional group; t = 7.37), not RCTs or meta-analyses. — [example SQ3R study](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/400600826_The_Effectiveness_of_Survey_Question_Read_Recite_and_Review_SQ3R_Technique_to_Increase_Students'_Reading_Comprehension)
- Anki (spaced retrieval software) in medical school: cohort studies show Anki users scoring 6.2%–12.9% higher on preclinical exams and a linear relation between mature-card count and scores; licensing-exam effects are small or null (USMLE Step 1 223.71 vs 222.58). These are observational, not randomized. — [Lu et al. 2023 cohort, Medical Science Educator](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10403443/); [Winter et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1177/23821205251369705)
- Duolingo's efficacy claims (five beginner units ≈ four university semesters for reading/listening; ~112 h vs ~240 h) come from company-funded white papers, one published in Foreign Language Annals; treat as vendor evidence. — [Duolingo efficacy whitepaper](https://duolingo-papers.s3.amazonaws.com/reports/duolingo-efficacy-whitepaper.pdf); [Duolingo blog on results](https://blog.duolingo.com/results-duolingo-efficacy-studies)

### Inferences
- For a book-grounded app the highest-yield primitives are: (1) retrieval questions generated from the passage the learner just read, (2) a spacing scheduler that re-asks across days with gaps scaled to the target horizon, (3) prompted self-explanation and "explain this back" tasks, (4) interleaved review mixing chapters/books, and (5) attempting a problem or prediction *before* reading the explanation (productive failure/generation). Highlighting and rereading can exist as UI affordances but should never be counted as "study" for progress.
- Summarization only helps when the summary gets feedback; an AI grader comparing a learner's summary to the cited passage converts a low-utility technique into a feedback-driven one (inference; no direct trial of AI-graded summaries found).
- The Anki evidence is dose-response and observational; the causal claim rests on the lab/classroom testing-effect literature, which is strong.

### Gaps
- I found no meta-analysis isolating effect sizes specifically for adult independent readers of trade non-fiction (nearly all evidence is textbook/course material with students).
- I could not locate a rigorous (RCT or meta-analytic) evaluation of SQ3R or of Gernsbacher's structure-building framework as an intervention; treat those as theory-motivated rather than evidence-graded.
- Dual coding and generation effect: not separately searched this session; Dunlosky 2013 covers imagery ("low" for text) and the generation effect is folded into practice testing/self-explanation evidence.

## Key Question 2: When do AI tutors help vs harm (2024–2026), and which guardrails flip harm into help?

### Takeaway
The pattern across 2024–2026 RCTs is consistent: unrestricted answer-giving AI raises performance while available and leaves learners flat or worse when it is removed (Bastani: −17% on the unassisted exam; Chen 2025: homework up, exam unchanged; Lehmann 2025: understanding harmed, gaps widened), whereas AI built with pedagogical constraints (hints not answers, step-by-step scaffolding, forced attempt first, structured usage) is neutral-to-strongly-positive (Bastani GPT Tutor: no harm; Kestin: d = 0.73–1.3 over active learning; Hou 2026: guided 0.86 SD vs unguided 0.09 SD). Engagement, not access, is the binding constraint in the field (Khanmigo two-year RCT).

### Cited Findings
- Bastani et al. 2025 (PNAS; ~1,000 Turkish high-school students, four 90-minute sessions over a semester; GPT Base = vanilla ChatGPT interface vs GPT Tutor = prompts with pedagogical guardrails giving hints without answers). Unfettered access improved practice performance but produced a 17% drop on the unassisted exam versus no-AI; GPT Tutor students performed the same as the textbook control on the exam (harm eliminated, no gain). — [PNAS article (403 to fetch)](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2422633122); numbers via [Stanford SCALE 2026 review PDF](https://scale.stanford.edu/sites/default/files/The%20Evidence%20Base%20on%20AI%20in%20K-12%20Report.pdf). The often-quoted practice-phase gains (GPT Base +48%, GPT Tutor +127%) are from the 2024 SSRN working-paper abstract and were not re-verified this session — [Semantic Scholar record](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Generative-AI-without-guardrails-can-harm-learning:-Bastani-Bastani/60570ad3268871882c65f6ed4ead35db4b220528). Training students with "adversarial examples" improved their ability to catch ChatGPT hallucinations. — [PNAS abstract via search](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2422633122)
- Kestin, Miller, Klales et al. 2025 (Scientific Reports; 194 Harvard intro-physics students, crossover across two topics, AI tutor vs best-practice active-learning class with identical worksheets): learning gains more than doubled (median post-test 4.5 vs 3.5), effect sizes 0.73–1.3 SD, done in ~49 min vs 60, higher engagement/motivation. The tutor was engineered on research-based principles (scaffolding, cognitive-load management, growth mindset, active engagement). — [Scientific Reports article (redirect on fetch)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-97652-6); [ETC Journal review](https://etcjournal.com/2025/11/10/review-of-kestin-et-al-s-june-2025-harvard-study-on-ai-tutoring/); [Impact AI Education explainer (blog)](https://impactaieducation.substack.com/p/a-harvard-randomized-controlled-trial)
- Fan et al. 2025 (BJET; 117 university students; ChatGPT vs human expert vs writing-analytics checklist vs none on an essay-revision task): ChatGPT group had the largest essay-score improvement but *no* difference in knowledge gain or transfer; no difference in post-task intrinsic motivation; the ChatGPT group showed fewer metacognitive processes (evaluation, orientation) and an SRL process network centered on ChatGPT, which the authors call "metacognitive laziness". — [Fan et al. BJET](https://bera-journals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/bjet.13544); [PDF](https://ttim.phbern.ch/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/Brit-J-Educational-Tech-2024-Fan-Beware-of-metacognitive-laziness-Effects-of-generative-artificial-intelligence-on.pdf); [Monash abstract](https://research.monash.edu/en/publications/beware-of-metacognitive-laziness-effects-of-generative-artificial/)
- Kosmyna et al. 2025 "Your Brain on ChatGPT" (arXiv preprint, not peer-reviewed; 54 participants, 18 in session 4): brain-only writers showed the strongest EEG connectivity, LLM users the weakest; 83% of LLM users failed to quote their own essay vs 11% in search/brain-only; lowest essay ownership in the LLM group; participants who wrote unaided first and then used the LLM retained stronger recall than the reverse order. — [arXiv 2506.08872](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.08872); quoting numbers via [SCALE 2026 review](https://scale.stanford.edu/sites/default/files/The%20Evidence%20Base%20on%20AI%20in%20K-12%20Report.pdf)
- Contractor & Reyes 2026 (arXiv; proctored in-person RCT, undergraduates learning unfamiliar material): AI access raised immediate knowledge-test scores by 0.27 SD, persisting at one week; "augmentation users" (conceptual explanations) had larger delayed gains, "automation users" (text generation) saw gains "vanish once AI is removed". The same paper cites Hou et al. 2026 (95 undergraduates): guided AI use with a structured prompting framework +0.86 SD on an unassisted post-test vs +0.09 SD unguided. — [arXiv 2607.08849](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08849)
- Stanford SCALE "The Evidence Base on AI in K-12: A 2026 Review" (Fesler, Martinez, Agnew, Loeb) concludes: tools "designed with pedagogical guardrails (such as AI chatbots for tutoring that provide step-by-step reasoning instead of direct answers) show more promise than general purpose AI tools"; performance gains while tools are available are common, but "effects when students no longer have tool access are mixed". Its RCT table includes: Chen et al. 2025 (155 US discrete-math students; LLM feedback+chatbot improved homework, no effect on unassisted exams); Lehmann et al. 2025 (postsecondary, Germany/NL; unrestricted ChatGPT-3.5 in Python had no overall effect, "harmed student understanding" and "widened achievement gaps for students with low prior knowledge"); Fischer et al. 2025 (334 German university students; unrestricted GPT-4+RAG chatbot beat 10-minute-delayed access, which beat textbook only, during a 25-min assisted session); Blasco & Charisi 2025 (122 Belgian/Spanish 13–15-year-olds; step-by-step-reasoning chatbot beat solution-only; Socratic chatbot increased engagement but was rated *less helpful* than a direct-answer one); Degen et al. 2025 (65 pre-service teachers; Socratic chatbot rated more supportive of critical, independent thinking); LearnLM/Eedi 2025 (UK; AI-guided tutors at least as good as human tutors with superior transfer). — [SCALE 2026 review PDF](https://scale.stanford.edu/sites/default/files/The%20Evidence%20Base%20on%20AI%20in%20K-12%20Report.pdf)
- SCALE follow-up brief "AI Tutoring is Not a Monolith" (Aug 20, 2026) argues evidence supports models keeping an adult in the loop and that fully automated tutoring "has not yet been tested at a standard that would settle the question". — [SCALE via search summary](https://scale.stanford.edu/ai/repository/student)
- Khanmigo two-year cluster RCT (Oreopoulos & Low, NBER w35620, Aug 2026; 18 Tennessee middle schools, daily remedial math, Khanmigo configured to coach not answer): +1.3 national percentile ranks per term, ~0.06–0.08 SD per school year; ~0.14 SD for full-year active participants, comparable to Khan Academy without AI; 96% tried it once but the median student messaged it on one-third of practice days and it was used in only 17% of error sessions; most messages were bare answers or clicks on suggested prompts. Authors: engagement is the primary constraint. — [NBER w35620](https://www.nber.org/papers/w35620). A separate undergraduate-physics mixed-methods study found no learning difference between Khanmigo and Google search. — [Journal of Teaching and Learning 2025](https://jtl.uwindsor.ca/index.php/jtl/article/view/10052)
- Tutor CoPilot (Wang, Ribeiro, Robinson, Loeb, Demszky; RCT with 900 tutors and 1,800 K-12 students): AI suggestions to *human* tutors raised topic mastery +4 pp overall, +9 pp for students of lower-rated tutors; tutors asked more guiding questions and gave away answers less; ~$20/tutor/year. — [arXiv 2410.03017](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.03017v1)
- Taipei RCT (10 high schools, 770 students, semester-long): LLM-guided RL personalization of practice *sequencing* improved an unassisted in-person exam by 0.15 SD over fixed sequencing, with no added instruction time. — [arXiv 2608.16907](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.16907)
- Meta-analyses of ChatGPT-in-education studies report large pooled effects (g = 0.573 in one; g = 0.867 for learning performance across 51 studies Nov 2022–Feb 2025 with >8-week interventions g = 1.054; g = 1.14 for undergraduates in another), but these pool mostly short, assisted-performance, quasi-experimental studies and should not be read as durable unassisted learning. — [ERIC EJ1501298](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1501298.pdf); [Wang & Fan 2025](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/391494932_The_effect_of_ChatGPT_on_students'_learning_performance_learning_perception_and_higher-order_thinking_insights_from_a_meta-analysis); [undergraduate meta-analysis](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2666920X25001766)
- Gerlich 2025 (Societies; 666 UK participants, survey + interviews, correlational): AI-tool use vs critical-thinking score r = −0.68; cognitive offloading correlated +0.72 with AI use and −0.75 with critical thinking; younger users most dependent. Correlational only. — [SSRN 5082524](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=5082524)
- Anthropic Education Report (Apr 8, 2025; 574,740 academic conversations from 1M): interactions split roughly evenly (~23–29% each) across direct problem solving, direct output creation, collaborative problem solving, collaborative output creation, i.e. ~47% "direct"; Claude was performing Creating (39.8%) and Analyzing (30.2%) far more than Remembering (1.8%); Anthropic flagged the risk that "AI systems may provide a crutch for students, stifling development of foundational skills". — [Anthropic Education Report, April 2025](https://www.anthropic.com/news/anthropic-education-report-how-university-students-use-claude). A Feb 23, 2026 follow-up ("AI Fluency Index") analysed 9,830 multi-turn conversations over 7 days in Jan 2026 across 11 collaboration behaviors. — [Claude Academy: AI Fluency Index](https://academy.claude.com/tutorials/the-ai-fluency-index)
- OpenAI Study Mode (launched late July/Aug 2025): Socratic-style questioning and hints, scaffolded sectioned responses, knowledge checks with feedback, personalization to skill level; built "with input from teachers, scientists, and pedagogy experts"; implemented via system instructions, not training; OpenAI published no outcome evaluation at launch and partnered with Stanford SCALE. On Mar 4, 2026 OpenAI announced a "Learning Outcomes Measurement Suite" (model behavior, learner response, cognitive outcomes over time) and said internal Study Mode research found "promising gains" without publishing numbers. — [OpenAI Study Mode announcement (403 on fetch)](https://openai.com/index/chatgpt-study-mode/); [InfoQ coverage](https://www.infoq.com/news/2025/08/study-mode-chatgpt); [OpenAI learning-outcomes post (403 on fetch; via search)](https://openai.com/index/understanding-ai-and-learning-outcomes/)
- OECD Digital Education Outlook 2026 (per search summary, not fetched): small-to-medium subject-learning gains and larger gains in critical thinking when AI tutoring follows process-oriented principles; gains "disappear or reverse when AI simply provides answers". — [OECD Digital Education Outlook 2026 PDF](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2026/01/oecd-digital-education-outlook-2026_940e0dd8/062a7394-en.pdf)
- Pre-LLM baseline: intelligent tutoring systems (50 controlled evaluations) raised scores a median 0.66 SD, larger on local than standardized tests. — [Kulik & Fletcher 2016, RER](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.3102/0034654315581420)
- Help-abuse in tutors: students viewed 68% of intermediate hint levels for under one second, clicking through to the bottom-out answer; help abuse correlates negatively with learning while time per hint level correlates positively, and help-seeking behavior explained as much variance in learning gains as cognitive performance. — [Aleven et al. "Help Helps, But Only So Much"](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40593-015-0089-1); [Baker, Corbett & Koedinger 2004 "Detecting Student Misuse"](http://pact.cs.cmu.edu/koedinger/pubs/Baker,%20Corbett,%20Koedinger%20ITS04.pdf)
- A 2026 arXiv paper proposes "auditable release control for pedagogical leakage" (detecting when an LLM tutor leaks full answers) as an engineering control. — [arXiv 2608.00515](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.00515)

### Inferences
- The guardrails with the best causal support are: never give the final answer before an attempt (Bastani GPT Tutor; Tutor CoPilot's "less likely to give away the answer"), step-by-step reasoning rather than solution dumps (Blasco & Charisi), structured usage protocols/prompt frameworks (Hou 2026), attempt-first ordering (Kosmyna session-4 crossover), and tutor design grounded in scaffolding and cognitive-load principles (Kestin).
- Socratic dialogue has a preference cost: learners rate it less helpful even when it engages them more, so a product needs mastery signals and explicit framing ("this is harder on purpose") to keep learners from routing around it. Bastani's adversarial-example training suggests teaching learners *why* answer-getting fails is itself an intervention.
- The Khanmigo trial shows that a well-guardrailed tutor that sits beside the work is barely used; a study home should make the tutor the path of least resistance inside the loop (retrieval question, hint, explain-back), not a side chat.
- Measure "unassisted" outcomes: any progress metric computed while the AI is open will replicate the assisted-performance illusion the literature warns about.

### Gaps
- No RCT found on LLM tutoring for adult independent readers of books outside a course; all durable-outcome RCTs are in courses or schools.
- No published outcome numbers for OpenAI Study Mode or Anthropic's Learning Mode; the Anthropic reports are usage analytics, not efficacy studies.
- Khanmigo NBER abstract does not report heterogeneity by prior achievement; Khan's internal "3+ sessions/week" claims are vendor reports (blog-level).

## Key Question 3: How should a product model progress honestly (mastery/knowledge tracing vs streaks), and what is known about calibration/JOL tooling?

### Takeaway
Mastery learning and knowledge tracing are the honest progress models: mastery learning has a replicated ~0.5 SD effect, learners need roughly seven quality practice opportunities per knowledge component to reach 80% accuracy (Koedinger 2023), and simple logistic/BKT models predict mastery well enough for scheduling; streaks/points show small, design-dependent effects and no evidence of measuring learning. Calibration is trainable (moderate effect across 56 studies) and cheap to instrument via confidence ratings and JOLs, which also mildly improve learning.

### Cited Findings
- Kulik, Kulik & Bangert-Drowns 1990 meta-analysis: mastery learning raised achievement 0.52 SD on average (0.57 on local tests, 0.29 on standardized tests). Bloom's 1984 "2 sigma" claim has never replicated; a 2020 review of 96 tutoring studies found none at 2 SD. — [Kulik et al. 1990 PDF](http://competencyworks.pbworks.com/w/file/fetch/70372726/1170612.pdf); [Education Next, Kraft on two-sigma](https://www.educationnext.org/two-sigma-tutoring-separating-science-fiction-from-science-fact/); [Nintil systematic review](https://nintil.com/bloom-sigma/)
- Koedinger, Carvalho, Liu & McLaughlin 2023 (PNAS; 27 datasets, >1.3M observations, 6,946 learners, 12 courses): median initial accuracy ≈ 65% on a knowledge component, median learning rate 0.09 log-odds per opportunity, ≈ 7.24 opportunities to reach 80% accuracy; students vary widely in initial knowledge but little in learning rate; "the main requirement for learning within educational technologies is additional opportunities." Replicated on further datasets and across demographic groups; a 2026 sensitivity analysis shows rate estimates depend on practice-sequence length; a blog critique argues the regularity is partly a modeling artifact. — [Koedinger et al. 2023](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/369381755_An_astonishing_regularity_in_student_learning_rate); [EDM 2024 replication](https://educationaldatamining.org/edm2024/proceedings/2024.EDM-short-papers.40/index.html); [demographic extension](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/378899535_Further_Evidence_for_Regularity_in_Student_Learning_Rates_Across_Demographic_Academic_Proficiency_and_Motivational_Groups); [sensitivity analysis arXiv 2605.01690](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.01690); [Skycak critique (blog)](https://www.justinmath.com/critique-of-paper-an-astonishing-regularity-in-student-learning-rate/)
- Gervet, Koedinger et al. 2020 (nine datasets): logistic regression with good features (LKT-family) wins on moderate-size datasets or many interactions per student; deep knowledge tracing wins on very large datasets or where timing matters; Bayesian Knowledge Tracing lags both. — [Gervet et al. 2020, JEDM](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1273917.pdf)
- Gamification meta-analysis (Sailer & Homner 2020; 38 publications): cognitive g = 0.49 (k = 19, N = 1,686), motivational g = 0.36 (k = 16), behavioral g = 0.25 (k = 9); success factors "still somewhat unresolved"; reward/status mechanics alone weakest, designs with genuine challenge, meaningful goals and narrative stronger. — [Sailer & Homner 2020, Educational Psychology Review](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s10648-019-09498-w.pdf)
- Metacognitive monitoring accuracy is trainable: a meta-analysis of 56 learning-strategy-instruction studies found a moderate improvement in calibration vs control. — ["Calibrating Calibration" meta-analysis](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/349179781_Calibrating_Calibration_A_Meta-Analysis_of_Learning_Strategy_Instruction_Interventions_to_Improve_Metacognitive_Monitoring_Accuracy)
- Repeated opportunities to self-evaluate against test performance improved calibration in an intro biology course; feedback on confidence-vs-performance improved both monitoring accuracy and performance in a training simulation; some general metacognitive training does not improve calibration. — [Osterhage et al., CBE Life Sciences Education](https://www.lifescied.org/doi/10.1187/cbe.18-10-0202); [Applied Ergonomics study](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0003687017302272); [Community College J. of Research and Practice](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/10668926.2020.1841042)
- JOLs made after a retrieval attempt are more accurate than after restudy because they draw on retrieval outcome, confidence and fluency. — [PMC6509741](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6509741/)
- Confidence-weighted multiple choice improves transfer vs standard MC (see KQ1). — [Sparck, Bjork & Bjork 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5256426/)
- Duolingo/Anki-style dose-response: mature Anki cards correlate linearly with exam scores (observational). — [Lu et al. 2023](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10403443/)

### Inferences
- Model progress per "knowledge component" (a concept/claim/passage-level skill extracted from the book) using a simple logistic learner model with opportunity counts, spacing features, and recent correctness; surface "N of ~7 successful retrievals, spaced" instead of percent-read or streak days. This matches the Koedinger opportunity finding and the Gervet result that logistic features beat BKT and, at small scale, DKT.
- Keep streaks (small behavioral effect) subordinate to a mastery view; never let a streak be earned by rereading or by AI-assisted answers, because that recreates the assisted-performance illusion.
- Instrument every retrieval with a 1–click confidence rating; show a calibration curve (confidence vs accuracy) per book and coach on overconfident items. Evidence supports both the learning benefit (small–medium) and trainability of calibration.

### Gaps
- No study found that evaluated a consumer-facing calibration dashboard for self-directed adult learners; evidence is from classroom/lab tasks.
- No head-to-head evidence on streaks vs mastery displays for retention/dropout in study apps (vendor data only).

## Key Question 4: What is known about planning, scheduling, goal-setting tools for self-directed learners, dropout, and motivation designs that do not cheapen learning?

### Takeaway
Self-directed learning has a brutal base rate (MOOC completion 3–15%; 52% never start), but cheap SRL scaffolds move it: open-ended planning prompts raised completion 29% and certificate payment 40%; implementation intentions have a pooled d = 0.65; utility-value reflections d ≈ 0.24 with larger effects for weaker students; autonomy-supportive designs correlate with performance and persistence across 200,000+ students; habits take a median 66 days (range 18–254) to become automatic, so scheduling must survive missed days.

### Cited Findings
- Zimmerman 2002 (Theory Into Practice 41:64–70) defines SRL as "the self-directive process by which learners transform their mental abilities into academic skills" through goal setting, strategy selection, monitoring, restructuring, time management, self-evaluation, and adaptation; the cyclical model has forethought, performance, and self-reflection phases. — [Zimmerman 2002 record](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Becoming-a-Self-Regulated-Learner:-An-Overview-Zimmerman/532c4ff8c63cfc711f824b52457a94f1016cc0d4)
- Reich & Ruipérez-Valiente 2019 (Science; 565 HarvardX/MITx MOOCs, 12.67M registrations, 6.5 years): completion 3–15%, unchanged over six years; 52% of registrants never start; most learners never return after year one. — [The MOOC Pivot](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/The-MOOC-pivot-Reich-Ruip%C3%A9rez-Valiente/fdd37727342a0da25294ba57953ea918665a5a2d); [Inside Higher Ed coverage](https://www.insidehighered.com/digital-learning/article/2019/01/16/study-offers-data-show-moocs-didnt-achieve-their-goals)
- Yeomans & Reich 2017 (three HarvardX courses, natural field experiment): open-ended planning prompts at course start raised completion 29% and certificate payment 40%, largest for learners enrolled in traditional schools; a 2024 follow-up found that plans specifying a time of day predict completion, but only 25% of learners used that tactic. — [Yeomans & Reich 2017](https://researchgate.net/publication/314101810_Planning_prompts_increase_and_forecast_course_completion_in_massive_open_online_courses); [Which Planning Tactics Predict Online Course Completion? LAK 2024](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3636555.3636891)
- A review of SRL interventions in MOOCs ("Promises and Pitfalls") documents mixed/small effects and implementation problems for generic SRL prompts. — [Technology, Knowledge and Learning 2021](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10758-021-09580-9)
- Gollwitzer & Sheeran 2006 meta-analysis: implementation intentions ("if situation X, then I do Y") d = 0.65 across 94 tests, >8,000 participants, including exam preparation and homework. — [meta-analysis explainer (blog)](https://goalsandprogress.com/implementation-intentions-gollwitzer-how-to/); mental-contrasting-with-implementation-intentions meta-analysis — [PMC8149892](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8149892/)
- Lally et al. 2010 (96 participants, 84 days of daily reports): median 66 days to 95% of asymptotic automaticity, range 18–254; missing one day did not materially derail formation. — [Lally et al. 2010, European J. Social Psychology](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/32898894_How_are_habits_formed_Modeling_habit_formation_in_the_real_world)
- Howard, Bureau, Guay, Chong & Ryan 2021 (344 samples, >200,000 students): autonomous motivation (intrinsic + identified) predicts performance, persistence, learning, satisfaction; identified regulation especially predicts persistence; autonomy support correlates positively with performance. — [Howard et al. 2021, Perspectives on Psychological Science](https://research-repository.uwa.edu.au/en/publications/student-motivation-and-associated-outcomes-a-meta-analysis-from-s/); [Bureau, Howard et al. 2021 RER pathways paper](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/09/2021_PathwaystoStudentMotiv_BureaHowardEtal.pdf)
- Utility-value interventions (writing about how material connects to one's life): meta-analysis of 33 randomized field studies, 12,478 participants, d = 0.24 on exams, grades, pass rates and interest; effects larger for low-performing students. — [Hulleman et al., "The Utility-Value Intervention"](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/341600326_The_Utility-Value_Intervention); [Hulleman et al. 2010 JEP original](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/232580960_Enhancing_Interest_and_Performance_With_a_Utility_Value_Intervention)
- Fan et al. 2025: none of the supports (ChatGPT, human expert, checklist) changed intrinsic motivation. — [Fan et al. BJET](https://bera-journals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/bjet.13544)
- Gamification: see KQ3 (Sailer & Homner 2020; motivational g = 0.36, reward/status mechanics weakest).
- Khanmigo RCT: 96% tried it once, median use one-third of practice days; access without engagement design yields ~0.06–0.08 SD/year. — [NBER w35620](https://www.nber.org/papers/w35620)

### Inferences
- The highest-evidence, lowest-cost planning features are: at book start, an open-ended plan prompt (when, where, how long); converting it to if-then implementation intentions with a time of day; a weekly plan that re-asks rather than punishes (missed days do not reset habit formation); and a short utility-value reflection ("why does this book matter to you?") stored and resurfaced.
- Motivation designs that do not cheapen learning: autonomy support (learner chooses goals, order, pace within a recommended plan), competence feedback tied to mastery evidence (retrieval success), relatedness via explain-to-someone tasks; avoid reward-only mechanics.
- Weekly cadence matters because MOOC dropout concentrates early; a first-week retrieval win plus a plan is the cheapest retention lever the evidence supports.

### Gaps
- Effect sizes for planning/scheduling tools specifically in self-study apps (not MOOCs) were not found.
- No Brazil-specific SRL/planning data found this session (the science is universal, as the brief assumes).

## Key Question 5: What does an evidence-based "study session" loop look like for a non-fiction chapter, a textbook chapter, and exam prep, and which product mechanisms have what strength of evidence?

### Takeaway
The loop the evidence supports is: forethought (goal + plan + prediction questions) → read one section with an attempt-before-explanation stance → immediate retrieval with confidence rating → self-explanation/explain-back graded against the cited passage → hints only after an attempt, never the answer first → reflection and scheduling of spaced, interleaved re-retrieval across chapters and books → unassisted checkpoint before "mastery" is granted.

### Cited Findings
- Attempt-then-instruct ordering: productive failure g = 0.36–0.58 (high fidelity) — [Sinha & Kapur 2021](https://janfasen.nl/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/Sinha-and-Kapur-PS-I.pdf); brain-only-then-LLM order retained recall better than LLM-then-brain-only — [Kosmyna et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.08872)
- Retrieval immediately after reading and again after delays: testing effect g ≈ 0.5–0.6 vs restudy; successive relearning +10% exam, retention >75% at one year with 56-day spacing. — [Adesope et al. 2017](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/315706448_Rethinking_the_Use_of_Tests_A_Meta-Analysis_of_Practice_Testing); [Janes et al. 2020](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/acp.3699)
- Spacing gaps ≈ 10–20% of the target retention interval. — [Cepeda et al. 2008](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED505660.pdf)
- Interleaving across topics/chapters d = 0.83 at one month. — [Rohrer et al. 2020](https://gwern.net/doc/psychology/spaced-repetition/2019-rohrer.pdf)
- Self-explanation prompts g = 0.55; teaching/explain-back g = 0.56 when the learner expects to teach and then teaches. — [Bisra et al. 2018](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1186664); [Kobayashi 2019](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jpr.12221)
- Confidence-weighted answers improve transfer; JOLs after retrieval improve calibration and learning. — [Sparck et al. 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5256426/); [PMC10607076](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10607076/)
- Hints that withhold the answer eliminate the AI harm (GPT Tutor); fast hint click-through predicts poor learning (68% of hint levels seen <1 s); time per hint level predicts learning. — [SCALE 2026 review on Bastani](https://scale.stanford.edu/sites/default/files/The%20Evidence%20Base%20on%20AI%20in%20K-12%20Report.pdf); [Aleven et al.](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40593-015-0089-1)
- Structured usage protocol for the AI (+0.86 SD vs +0.09 unguided). — [Hou et al. 2026 via Contractor & Reyes](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08849)
- Research-principled tutor design beats active-learning class (d = 0.73–1.3) in a course context. — [Kestin et al. 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-97652-6)
- ~7 quality opportunities per knowledge component to reach 80%. — [Koedinger et al. 2023](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/369381755_An_astonishing_regularity_in_student_learning_rate)
- Planning prompts and if-then plans for the forethought phase (+29% completion; d = 0.65). — [Yeomans & Reich 2017](https://researchgate.net/publication/314101810_Planning_prompts_increase_and_forecast_course_completion_in_massive_open_online_courses); [Gollwitzer & Sheeran 2006 (blog summary)](https://goalsandprogress.com/implementation-intentions-gollwitzer-how-to/)
- Unassisted assessment is the only honest signal: assisted-practice gains repeatedly failed to transfer to unassisted exams. — [SCALE 2026 review](https://scale.stanford.edu/sites/default/files/The%20Evidence%20Base%20on%20AI%20in%20K-12%20Report.pdf)

### Inferences (product mechanisms with evidence strength)
- Strong (multiple RCTs/meta-analyses): forced retrieval before reveal; spaced re-retrieval scheduler across chapters and books; interleaved review sets; self-explanation prompts; explain-back/Feynman with an announced "you will teach this" expectancy; problem/prediction before explanation; hints-not-answers with attempt gating; unassisted mastery checkpoints; planning prompt + if-then schedule at book start.
- Medium (meta-analysis of instruction studies or single strong RCT): confidence ratings with calibration feedback; utility-value reflection; per-component mastery model (logistic KT); structured "how to use the tutor" protocol; step-by-step scaffolding instead of full solutions; delayed hint levels with minimum dwell time.
- Weak or design-dependent: Socratic-only dialogue as the sole mode (engagement up, perceived helpfulness down; no durable-outcome RCT isolating it); streaks/badges (small behavioral effect, no learning measurement); AI-graded summaries (plausible from feedback literature, untested); rereading/highlighting as tracked activity (low utility).
- Session templates implied by the evidence:
  - Non-fiction chapter (≈30–45 min): 2-min goal + prediction questions from headings → read one section → 3–5 retrieval items with confidence → one self-explanation ("why does the author claim X?") graded against cited passage → hints only after attempt → 1-min reflection → scheduler enqueues items at ~10–20% of the horizon and mixes them with prior chapters.
  - Textbook chapter: same, plus worked-example fading (attempt problem → step-by-step scaffold only on failure), interleaved problem sets from earlier chapters, and a weekly unassisted quiz that alone updates mastery.
  - Exam prep: build a knowledge-component map from the syllabus/book; successive relearning to criterion (each item retrieved correctly on ≥3 spaced days); calibration review of overconfident misses; interleaved mixed-topic mocks; AI restricted to hint/explanation after attempt, never answer-first; final unassisted mock predicts readiness.

### Gaps
- No trial combines all of these mechanisms in one consumer app for adult book learners; the composite loop is an inference from component effects, which may not add linearly.
- No evidence found on the optimal amount of AI explanation per session, or on whether AI-generated retrieval questions match the quality of human-authored ones for the testing effect.
- Long-context "teach me this passage" (Learny's teaching sessions) has no direct RCT; the closest evidence is Kestin's principled tutor (positive) versus Bastani's GPT Base (negative), which differ mainly in guardrails.
